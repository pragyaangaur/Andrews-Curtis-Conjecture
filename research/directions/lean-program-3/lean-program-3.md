# Direction F5: Lean program 3

Round 3 deliverable for direction F5. The three targets are the cocommutativity kill theorem, the
permuted-order variant of Lemma 8, and a reusable certificate verifier. The formalization is in
`toolkit/lean/AC3.lean`, which imports only `Mathlib` and is checked with one command. This report
gives the statements in English, the exact commands, the output that matters, and the limits.

Labels. **Proved** means a Lean 4 theorem with no `sorry`, no new axiom and no `native_decide`,
whose `#print axioms` output is quoted below. **Measured** means a command whose output is quoted.
**Argument** means a proof written out in this report and not formalized. **Reported** means a
statement taken from a file of the campaign without reproof here.

Nothing in this direction proves or disproves the Andrews-Curtis conjecture or its stable form.

## 1. Outcome

1. **(a) The cocommutativity kill is machine-checked, in the unstable and the stable form.**
   A presentation is cocommutative when every relator has zero exponent sum in every generator.
   No cocommutative tuple AC-equivalent to a balanced presentation of the trivial group exists at
   positive rank (`not_cocommutative_of_acEquiv`), and no cocommutative tuple of rank `m ≥ 1`
   stably AC-equivalent to such a presentation exists either (`not_cocommutative_of_stablyACEquiv`).
   The stable statement is for the move set generated by AC moves, stabilization by an arbitrary
   word (`stabWith`), and the inverse of such a stabilization. The word-one case of that move is
   the balance-preserving stabilization of the Shehper move set; the general case adds the
   generator `x_n` with the relator `x_n * w`.
2. **(b) The permuted-order variant of Lemma 8 is machine-checked**
   (`acEquiv_standard_of_generator_relators_perm`), together with the ingredient its proof needs:
   any permutation of the relator positions is an AC-equivalence (`acEquiv_permute`). The item is
   listed as not formalized in the ledger of `AC2.lean`.
3. **(c) A reusable certificate verifier is machine-checked and exercised.**
   `Move`, `replay`, `checkCert`, `checkCert_sound` replay an explicit move list in the kernel, and
   `checkCert_append` composes block checks so that long chains do not exceed the elaborator's
   limits. `toolkit/lean/cert_to_lean.py` converts both certificate formats of the campaign into
   Lean, `check_cert_lean.sh` checks one certificate, `check_ms_certs.sh` checks all of them.
   Measured: all eighteen certificates of `directions/ms-ak-certs/` check in one Lean run, and
   thirty-four certificates of `directions/c14/certs_all.txt`, spread over the whole file and
   including the longest one (141 moves, line 53850), check in another.

## 3. The statements, in English

All names are in `namespace AndrewsCurtis`.

* `Cocommutative P` means `∀ (i a : Fin n), expSumAt a (P i) = 1`: every relator has zero exponent
  sum in every generator.
* `cocommutative_invertAt`, `cocommutative_conjAt`, `cocommutative_mulAt`,
  `Cocommutative.of_acMove`, `Cocommutative.of_acEquiv`: cocommutativity is invariant under each
  of the three moves and under AC-equivalence. Inversion negates the exponent sums and conjugation
  does not change them, so a vanishing sum stays zero.
* `exists_cocommutative_acEquiv_iff (P) : (∃ Q, ACEquiv P Q ∧ Cocommutative Q) ↔ Cocommutative P`.
  The AC class of a tuple meets the cocommutative tuples exactly when the tuple is itself
  cocommutative. No hypothesis on the presented group.
* `not_cocommutative_of_subsingleton (hn : 0 < n) (P) (htriv : Subsingleton (PresentedGroup (Set.range P))) :
  ¬ Cocommutative P`. The engine: if every other relator has zero exponent sum in `x_i` and the
  presentation is of the trivial group, then the exponent sum of `x_i` in the `i`-th relator is
  `±1` (`expSum_eq_pm_one`, already in `AC2.lean`); cocommutativity forces it to be `0`, and
  `0 = ±1` is impossible in `Multiplicative ℤ`.
* `not_cocommutative_of_acEquiv (hn : 0 < n) (htriv) (h : ACEquiv P Q) : ¬ Cocommutative Q`.
  Theorem 1 of `directions/invariant-ideas/idea.md`, unstable form.
* `exists_expSum_ne_one (hn : 0 < n) (P) (htriv) : ∃ (i a : Fin n), expSumAt a (P i) ≠ 1`.
  The explicit form: some relator has a nonzero exponent sum.
* `permute P π` relabels the relator positions by `π : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`;
  `permute_mul_swap` identifies one transposition of the entries with the multiplication of `π` by
  `Equiv.swap x y`; `acEquiv_permute P π : ACEquiv P (permute P π)`.
* `acEquiv_standard_of_generator_relators_perm (P) (i) (π) (hgen) (htriv) :
  ACEquiv P (standard n)`, where `hgen : ∀ j ≠ i, P (π j) = FreeGroup.of j ∨ P (π j) = (FreeGroup.of j)⁻¹`.
  Lemma 8 of `survey/q-transformations.md` with the generator relators in a permuted order.
  `acEquiv_standard_of_generator_relators_via_perm` checks that the identity permutation gives
  exactly the statement of `AC2.lean`.
* `Move n` is one recorded move: `invert i`, `conj i w`, `mul i j`; `Move.apply` returns the new
  tuple, and `Move.apply_sound` says a successful application is an `ACEquiv` step. `replay ms P`
  replays a list, `replay_sound` turns it into an `ACEquiv`, `checkCert ms start finish` is the
  Boolean `decide (replay ms start = some finish)`, and `checkCert_sound` extracts the
  equivalence. `checkCert_append` composes two blocks through an intermediate tuple.
* `stabWith P w` adds one generator together with the relator `x_n * w`; `stab P = stabWith P 1`.
  `subsingleton_presentedGroup_of_stabWith` and `subsingleton_of_subsingleton_stabWith` say that
  triviality of the presented group is preserved in both directions. The forward direction sends
  everything out of the stabilized free group through the old presented group, which is trivial;
  the backward direction sends the new generator to `(w)⁻¹` in the old presented group, so the new
  relator dies.
* `StableMove` is the inductive relation on `Σ n, Presentation n` with the three constructors `ac`,
  `stab`, `destab`; `StablyACEquiv` is its reflexive transitive closure;
  `subsingleton_iff_of_stableMove` and `subsingleton_of_stablyACEquiv` transport triviality along a
  stable equivalence.
* `not_cocommutative_of_stablyACEquiv (hm : 0 < m) (htriv : Subsingleton (PresentedGroup (Set.range P)))
  (h : StablyACEquiv ⟨n, P⟩ ⟨m, Q⟩) : ¬ Cocommutative Q`. Theorem 1 of `idea.md`, stable form,
  with the rank hypothesis `m ≥ 1` that its corrected statement carries.
* `stab_standard : stab (standard n) = standard (n+1)`, and the instance
  `example (m) : ¬ Cocommutative (standard (m+1))`, which shows the hypotheses are satisfiable.

## 5. Limits, and what is not verified

1. **`Cocommutative` is defined by vanishing exponent sums, and the equivalence with "every relator
   lies in the commutator subgroup" is not formalized.** The step would need the abelianisation of
   the free group as a homomorphism onto `Z^n` and the identification of the commutator subgroup
   with its kernel. This is standard and is the only gap between the Lean predicate and the
   hypothesis of Barmak's invariant; the kill itself uses only the predicate.
2. **Theorem 2 of `directions/invariant-ideas/idea.md` is not formalized.** That theorem identifies
   Barmak's general winding invariant on the class of interest with the exponent-sum matrix. Only the
   killing predicate is formalized.
3. **The stable move set.** `StableMove` has AC moves, stabilization by a word, and the exact
   inverse of a stabilization. The general destabilization, which deletes a generator occurring in
   only one relator and only as a conjugate of itself, is not formalized, so `StablyACEquiv` is a
   sub-relation of the relation generated by the general stable moves; the kill proved here is the
   corresponding restriction. The deficiency-changing MMS move (add a free generator, no relator) is
   not formalized either: it changes the presented group, so no triviality-preservation argument
   applies to it, and nothing here covers the relation generated by that move together with AC
   moves. This is exactly the move-set distinction that direction F2 is asked to settle; see
   section 6.
4. **One source of cost, recorded exactly.** A single `decide` over the 141-move certificate failed
   at the default `maxRecDepth` (1000) with `maximum recursion depth has been reached`, and with
   `maxRecDepth 100000` and `maxHeartbeats 4000000` it still failed at the recursion limit after
   about 73 s. Blocking the chain into pieces of ten with `checkCert_append` removes the failure: the
   same certificate now checks in about 13 s. The blocking is what makes the verifier usable on the
   longest certificates in the campaign.
5. **`Classical.choice` appears in the axiom list of the certificate checks.** It comes from the
   `DecidableEq` instance for `FreeGroup`, which goes through `FreeGroup.toWord`. The check is still
   a kernel computation: Lean accepts the proof only if `decide` reduces to `true`, and
   `#print axioms` confirms that no `sorryAx` and no new axiom is involved. The kernel whose
   acceptance was required is Lean's own.
6. **The linter warning** at the copied line is not an error and does not indicate an unfinished
   proof. It is inherited from `AC2.lean`, which carries the same warning.
7. **Nothing here is about the conjecture.** The kill theorem rules out a class of tuples as
   *starting points* of a winding-invariant argument; it says nothing about whether AK(3) is
   AC-trivial.

## 6. Cross-direction notes

1. For direction F2 (the two stabilization move sets): the formalization makes the distinction
   exact. The Shehper move, which adds a generator together with the relator equal to it, preserves
   the presented group, and the slightly more general `stabWith P w`, which adds the relator
   `x_n * w`, does too; both are covered by `subsingleton_..._stabWith` here. The MMS move that adds
   a free generator and no relator does not preserve the presented group: the new presentation is
   the free product of the old group with `Z`, so triviality is destroyed unless the old group was
   the free group. Consequently no triviality-based invariant can be transported across that move,
   and the two relations cannot be equal on the class of balanced presentations of the trivial group
   for any invariant of this kind. This is an argument, not a formalized theorem, and it does not
   settle F2's question, which is about the equivalence relations themselves.
2. For direction F9 (second referees): the eighteen `directions/ms-ak-certs` certificates and the
   longest `directions/c14` certificate now have an independent Lean check, separate from the
   Python replays of Round 2.
