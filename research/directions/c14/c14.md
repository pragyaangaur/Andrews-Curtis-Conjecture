## 1. Conventions

The rank-2 free group is on `a` and `b`. A word is a tuple of integers with
`1 = a`, `-1 = a^-1`, `2 = b` and `-2 = b^-1`, kept freely reduced. A
presentation is a pair of words `(r1, r2)`. The three AC moves are inversion of
one relator, multiplication of one relator on the right by the other, and
conjugation of one relator by a generator or its inverse. Conjugation by a longer
word is a succession of these moves, as Havas and Ramsay note.

Two presentations are identified when one is obtained from the other by
reordering the relators (notational), cyclically permuting a relator (conjugation
by a prefix, hence an AC move) or inverting a relator (an AC move). The canonical
form of a presentation is: each relator freely and cyclically reduced and minimal
under cyclic permutation and inversion, the pair ordered by (length, word). This
is the normal form used by `survey/computational/enum_check.py` in round 1, and
the code here reproduces the published list sizes with it.

The total relator length of a pair is the sum of the two relator lengths. For a
relation written `u = v` this is `|u| + |v|`, the length of the relator `u v^-1`.

AK(3) is `<x,y | xyx = yxy, x^3 = y^4>`. In the letter notation above its relator
pair is `(aaaBBBB, abaBAB)` and its canonical form is `(BABaba, BBBBaaa)`. That
AK(3) presents the trivial group is machine-checked in
`directions/ak-status/ak_trivial.lean` from round 1, and nothing in this file
depends on that fact.

The ball of a pair `s` with cap `C` is the set of pairs reachable from `s` by AC
moves with every intermediate pair of total relator length at most `C`. Depth is
breadth-first distance in that capped graph.

## 3. How each presentation is classified

One presentation `t` is classified by these steps, and the first that applies is
recorded.

1. `full_reduce(t)` repeatedly applies the containment reduction of step (C) and
   returns a shorter pair together with the explicit AC moves that perform the
   reduction. The reduction is a proof and not a heuristic: if a rotation of `r1`
   is a subword `x` of a rotation of `r2`, then conjugation and multiplication
   turn `r2` into the complement of `x`, shorter by `|x|`. The moves are emitted
   and replayed like any other certificate.
2. `certificate(t, ball)` looks for a cyclic-permutation or inversion variant of
   the reduced pair inside the ball of the standard pair `(a, b)` with cap `C`.
   When it finds one it descends the breadth-first tree to `(a, b)` and returns
   an explicit list of elementary AC moves from `t` to `(a, b)`.
3. Otherwise the group is tested by a Todd-Coxeter enumeration of the cosets of
   the trivial subgroup with a 20000-coset limit (`tc.py`), and a search over the
   perfect groups `A5`, `SL(2,5)` and `PSL(2,7)` looks for a homomorphism whose
   image is nontrivial (`quotient_witness`). Such a homomorphism is a complete
   certificate that the presented group is not trivial, hence that the
   presentation is not AC-trivial. The determinant condition forces the presented
   group to be perfect, so no other finite group can be a witness.
4. Otherwise the presentation is recorded as resisting, with the bound of the
   search that was run.

The balls used, from the standard pair, have 700712 pairs at cap 11, 1582280 at
cap 12, 6476104 at cap 13, 14399816 at cap 14 and 58487624 at cap 15. The cap-11
and cap-13 sizes agree with round 1. From AK(3) the balls have 168 pairs at cap
14, 6864 at cap 15, 20560 at cap 16 and 857808 at cap 17. **Computed.**

For presentations that survive step 2 with cap 15, a meet-in-the-middle search is
run (`stage_mitm2.py`). It explores the whole component of the presentation
inside total relator length at most `C`, breadth first and without a depth limit,
and looks for any pair on the frontier that lies in the cap-14 ball of the
standard pair or in the cap-17 ball of AK(3). When the exploration is exhaustive
it decides the question completely for that cap, because a proof in which every
intermediate pair has total length at most `C` must pass through the standard
pair and therefore through a pair of the cap-14 ball. **Argument.** This is the
only place where the classification rests on reasoning rather than on a replayed
computation, and the reasoning is written out here so that it can be checked.

## 5. Certificates and the independent checker

A certificate is one line of `certs_all.txt`:

```
start|moves|end
```

with `start` and `end` of the form `word,word` over the letters `a`, `A`, `b`,
`B`, and `moves` a space-separated list of tokens `i0`, `i1` (invert a relator),
`c0a`, `c1B` (conjugate a relator by one generator) and `m0`, `m1` (multiply a
relator by the other). The end is `std` for the pair `(a,b)`, or the literal
relator pair of AK(3). A certificate whose start is the swap of the two relators
of the census presentation is legitimate because the relator order is notational
and the move set is symmetric in it; this happens for 26343 of the 53866 lines.
The checker requires the replay to end exactly at the stated end pair, so a swap
at the start is visible in the line and cannot be missed.

`replay14.py` is the checker. It was written from scratch for this direction:
its own parser, its own free reduction, its own inverse, product and conjugation
routines, and no import from any other file of this direction. On the full
certificate file it reports

```
replayed 53866 certificates, 0 failed, longest 141 moves
```

**Computed.** The 53866 lines are the 53840 AC-trivial certificates and the 26
AK(3)-equivalence certificates, and all lines are distinct.

## 6. The resisting set and its exact bounds

164 presentations are not decided, and their class labels carry the bound:

* `resisting-exhausted-cap17` (34), `resisting-exhausted-cap18` (40) and
  `resisting-exhausted-cap19` (68). For these the whole component inside total
  relator length at most the named cap was explored, and it contains no pair of
  the cap-14 ball of the standard pair and no pair of the cap-17 ball of AK(3).
  By the argument of Section 3 there is no AC proof from such a presentation to
  the standard pair in which every intermediate pair has total relator length at
  most that cap, and no AC-equivalence to AK(3) whose intermediate pairs all stay
  within that cap while passing through the cap-17 ball of AK(3).
* `resisting-budget-cap19` (22). The exploration was stopped after 400000
  distinct pairs at cap 19. These presentations may still be AC-trivial, and a
  larger cap or budget could settle them.

The exploration budgets are explicit: 250000 pairs at cap 17, 500000 at cap 18
and 400000 at cap 19. A presentation that was exhausted at a low cap and then
stopped by the budget at a higher cap is labelled by the low cap, which is the
stronger statement. **Computed.**

For orientation, the published record is that Havas and Ramsay reduce all but
twelve of the 1400 presentations of their length-13 list once relators are
allowed to grow to length eighteen. This run's bound is a cap on the total
relator length rather than on a single relator, and the 164 undecided
presentations are exactly those for which the cap-19 component is large. No claim
is made that any of them is a counterexample, and no claim is made that any of
them is a presentation of a nontrivial group.

## 7. The two pruning steps of the published enumeration

Step (C) discards a presentation when one relator is a cyclic subword of the
other. The reduction is constructive, so every discarded presentation is
certified AC-trivial or AC-equivalent to AK(3) through the composed certificate.
**Computed** for all lengths from 12 to 14.

Step (D) discards a presentation when a relator reads `x_i = x_j^m` up to cyclic
permutation and inversion. This step is not obviously an AC reduction: the
relation `b = a^m` does not delete the generator `b` by an AC move, and removing
`b` from the other relator needs a search. Havas and Ramsay justify both steps in
one sentence. This run therefore classified the step (D) discards as ordinary
presentations, and the outcome is:

* length 13: 592 discards, all 592 AC-trivial with explicit certificates;
* length 14: 648 discards, all 648 AC-trivial with explicit certificates.

So at these lengths the pruning is harmless and the published length-13 list
describes every AC class at that length. The general statement that the step (D)
discards are always AC-trivial is not proved here and is left open.
**Computed** at the two lengths, **open** in general.

## 8. The Miller-Schupp slice at length 14

Shehper et al. report a search over the presentations

```
MS(n,w) = <a,b | a^-1 b^n a = b^(n+1), a = w>
```

with `n <= 7` and `|w| <= 7`, a family they count as 1190 presentations, of which
six at length 14 resisted their greedy search; four of the six were reported to be
AC-equivalent to AK(3). This section reports what the census of this file says
about that family.

I could not reproduce the count 1190 from the definition. With `n <= 7` and
`|w| <= 7` the family has 4843 canonical presentations when every word `w` is
allowed and 889 when the exponent sum of `w` on `a` is required to be zero; before
the symmetry reduction the counts are 30611 and 4557, and with the total length
also fixed at 14 the counts are 191 and 41. None of these is 1190. The parameter
range or the identification used in the paper is therefore not settled here, and
the comparison below is made on the total-length-14 slice rather than against
their list.

Of the 191 canonical presentations of total length 14 in this slice, 51 have
unimodular abelianisation and so lie in the census. Their classes are 45
AC-trivial, one AC-equivalent to AK(3) and five resisting, and the resisting ones
are

```
BBBAbba,BAAAbaa    n=2  w = a^-2 b a^2 b^-1      exhausted at cap 18
BBBAbba,BAAbaaa    n=2  w = a^-2 b^-1 a^2 b      exhausted at cap 18
BBBAbba,BAABaaa    n=2  w = a^-2 b a^2 b         exhausted at cap 19
BBBAbba,BAAABaa    n=2  w = a^-2 b^-1 a^2 b^-1   exhausted at cap 19
BAABa,BBBBAbbba    n=3  w = b a^2 b             exhausted at cap 18
```

(all in the canonical letter notation, with `w` given for one representing word).
The sixth member of the slice that resisted the cap-18 search was
`BABaa,BBBBAbbba`, which is `MS(3, y^-1 x^2 y^-1)`; the cap-19 pass produced an
explicit 34-move certificate taking it to the relator pair of AK(3), which
replays. **Computed.**

Two of the six at the cap-18 stage are the presentations for which Carreras,
arXiv:2607.23611, claims explicit certificates of AC-equivalence to AK(3) with
move counts 66 and 13, namely the two with `n = 3` (reading the four counts in
the order of the four claims of that abstract). The 13-move claim concerns
`MS(3, y^-1 x^2 y^-1)`, which this run now also certifies, with 34 moves rather
than 13; Carreras's certificate was not read or replayed. The 66-move claim
concerns `MS(3, y x^2 y)`, which is `BAABa,BBBBAbbba`; this run does not resolve
it, and its bound is a cap-18 exhaustion, so a 66-move proof that grows relators
beyond 8 letters is outside the bound and the two statements are compatible. The
six found here are consistent with the published report of six, but this file
does not identify its six with the six of Shehper et al. and does not verify the
four AK(3)-equivalences they report. **Reported**, not verified.

## 9. What is not claimed

1. Nothing here is evidence for or against the conjecture. AK(3) resists every
   search run in this file, which is what the published record already says, and
   that is a bounded negative and not a result about AK(3).
2. The 164 resisting presentations are not claimed to be nontrivial, not claimed
   to be presentations of the trivial group, and not claimed to be
   counterexamples. For all of them the Todd-Coxeter enumeration closed with one
   coset, so each does present the trivial group, but that computation carries a
   20000-coset limit and is labelled accordingly.
3. The completeness of the enumeration rests on the pruning steps of Section 7.
   Step (B) is sound because a non-unimodular exponent-sum matrix means a
   nontrivial abelianisation. Step (C) is sound by an explicit reduction. Step (D)
   is checked only at lengths 13 and 14.
4. The comparison with Shehper et al. is a comparison of counts on a slice that I
   could not match to their stated 1190, and the comparison with Carreras is a
   comparison of statements, not a replay of their certificates.
5. The certificates are all in one move system, the twelve restricted moves with
   conjugation by one generator. A certificate in this system is a certificate in
   the full AC system, since each restricted move is an AC move.
