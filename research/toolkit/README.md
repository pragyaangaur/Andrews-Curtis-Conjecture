# Andrews-Curtis moves in Python

This directory holds a small toolkit for the three Andrews-Curtis moves on
balanced presentations of the trivial group, together with a deterministic
breadth-first search over bounded families of presentations and a self check
that prints one PASS line per test.

The point of the toolkit is that it certifies what it says.  Every presentation
that the search reports as reachable from the standard presentation comes with
a list of moves, and that list is replayed one move at a time by
`ac.verify_path`.  A reader who trusts the arithmetic of `words.py` and the
move table of `ac.py` can check the assertions of the runs below by replaying
the certificates, and the self check compares the arithmetic against sympy's
free groups.

Everything is deterministic.  There is no random number generator anywhere
except `random.Random(20261104)` inside the test script, and no dependence on
dict or hash ordering: every frontier is sorted before it is expanded.

## Files

| file | contents |
| --- | --- |
| `words.py` | reduced words in F_n: reduction, inverse, product, conjugation, commutator, exponent sums, cyclic permutation, printing and parsing, enumeration of all reduced words below a length bound, exact determinant, Smith elementary divisors |
| `ac.py` | presentations as tuples of relators, the three moves as tagged functions, successor generation, path verification, the exponent-sum matrix and its normal form, the Akbulut-Kirby tuple |
| `bfs.py` | breadth-first search from the standard presentation with a relator length bound and a conjugator length bound, path reconstruction, full replay of every stored path, comparison with the whole bounded box, export and replay of certificate files, command line report |
| `test_toolkit.py` | the self check, 144 checks, exit status 0 only if all pass |
| `runs/` | recorded output of the commands quoted in this file |
| `runs/gap_check.py` | the extra script quoted at the end, which compares two relator length bounds |

## Conventions

A word in the free group F_n is a tuple of nonzero integers.  The letter `i`
is the generator x_i and the letter `-i` is x_i^{-1}, so that `(1, 2, -1)` is
x_1 x_2 x_1^{-1}.  A word is reduced when no entry is the negation of the next
entry, and `words.reduce_word` returns the reduced representative of any tuple.
Generator labels and relator positions are 1-based, matching the notation
x_1, ..., x_n and r_1, ..., r_n.  `words.format_word` prints generators as x, y,
z and inverses as X, Y, Z, so AK(3) prints as `(xyxYXY, xxxYYYY)`.

A presentation is a tuple `rs` of n reduced relators.  The three moves are
carried by tags

* `('inv', i)`, which replaces r_i by r_i^{-1},
* `('mul', i, j)`, which replaces r_i by r_i r_j for i different from j,
* `('conj', i, w)`, which replaces r_i by w r_i w^{-1} for a word w.

A list of tags is a certificate of AC-equivalence when it replays from the
starting presentation, and `ac.verify_path` performs the replay.  The move set
of the problem statement uses right multiplication only.  Left multiplication
is available as two forward moves, conjugation by r_j followed by right
multiplication by r_j, and the inverse of a right multiplication by r_j is
three forward moves.  Consequently each move can be undone by forward moves,
so the set of presentations reachable from the standard presentation by
directed moves is the whole AC-equivalence class of the standard presentation.
The test script checks all three undoings.

## One invariant, with proof

Let E(P) be the n by n matrix whose i-th row is the exponent sum vector of r_i
in Z^n.  Under the moves E(P) is multiplied on the left by an element of
GL_n(Z).  Move `('inv', i)` negates row i.  Move `('mul', i, j)` adds row j to
row i.  Move `('conj', i, w)` changes nothing, because the exponent sum map is a
homomorphism to the abelian group Z^n and therefore sends w r_i w^{-1} to
exp(w) + exp(r_i) - exp(w).  Hence the subgroup of Z^n generated by the rows,
and with it the Smith normal form of E(P), is unchanged by every AC move, in
both directions of the equivalence relation.

The standard presentation has E equal to the identity matrix.  Every
presentation AC-equivalent to the standard one therefore has E in GL_n(Z), that
is, determinant plus or minus one.  Taking the contrapositive gives the one
unconditional negative test in the toolkit: a presentation whose exponent-sum
matrix has determinant other than plus or minus one is not AC-equivalent to the
standard presentation.  For example, `(x, x)` has exponent-sum matrix with
determinant 0, so it is not AC-equivalent to `(x, y)`, with no bound anywhere in
the argument.  The test script checks the invariance on every edge of the rank
2 search at relator length 3, which is 8512 edges.

The determinant test is far from a complete test of AC-trivialisability.  Every
balanced presentation of the trivial group has a unimodular exponent-sum matrix,
so the test cannot separate any of them from the standard presentation, and the
conjecture says that all of them are in fact reachable.

The search uses the invariant as a prune.  The prune is sound for the search
because every presentation reachable from the standard one has a unimodular
exponent-sum matrix, so a path from the standard presentation never passes
through a pruned vertex.

`runs/`.

```
python3 toolkit/test_toolkit.py
```

144 checks, exit status 0, about ten seconds on this machine.  The full output is
`runs/test_output.txt` and the last line is

### Robustness in the conjugator bound

```
python3 toolkit/bfs.py --relator-len 5 --conj-len 3 --box-gap --gap-examples 2
```

gives the same component of 31240 presentations, the same 800 missed
presentations, and the same length profile `[5, 5]: 800`, in 9.89 seconds.  The
conjugator bound is therefore not what limits Search B.  The self check also
records that the components at conjugator lengths 1 and 2 agree for rank 2 with
relator length 3 and for rank 3 with relator length 2.

### Certificate files

The results of a search can be written out and checked later without running a
search.  Each line of a certificate file holds one presentation and a list of
moves that reaches it from the standard presentation.

```
python3 toolkit/bfs.py --relator-len 4 --conj-len 2 --export cert.txt
python3 toolkit/bfs.py --rank 2 --replay cert.txt
```

The first command writes 2280 lines and immediately replays them.  The second
command reads the file and replays every line using only `words.py` and `ac.py`.
Its output for the search quoted above is

```
certificate file cert.txt at rank 2
lines that replay from the standard presentation = 2280
lines that fail = 0
```

A line looks like `Y;YYX<TAB>mul 1 2, mul 2 1, inv 2, mul 1 2, conj 2 Y`, where
the part before the tab is the target presentation in the notation of
`words.format_word` with relators separated by semicolons, and the part after
the tab is the move list with moves separated by commas.

## What these runs establish

Each item below is a finite computation that anyone can rerun with the command
quoted above.  The certificates are not trusted: `bfs.verify_all_paths` replays
every stored path through `ac.apply_move`, which validates each tag.

**Proposition 1.**  Let P be a presentation with two relators, each a reduced
word of length at most 4 in F_2.  Then P is AC-equivalent to the standard
presentation `(x, y)` if and only if the exponent-sum matrix of P has
determinant plus or minus one.

**Proposition 2.**  Let P be a presentation with three relators, each a reduced
word of length at most 2 in F_3.  Then P is AC-equivalent to the standard
presentation `(x, y, z)` if and only if the exponent-sum matrix of P has
determinant plus or minus one.

In both cases the "only if" direction is the invariant lemma above and holds for
every presentation at every length.  The "if" direction is the computation: the
bounded box contains 2280 presentations in the first case and 8112 in the
second, the search reaches every one of them, and the 10392 certificates replay.
The largest distance from the standard presentation is 8 in the first case and
11 in the second.

I do not know whether these two statements appear in the literature.  They are
small enough that they may be folklore, and the search bounds are far below what
is needed for any conjecture of interest.  The value of the two statements for
this project is that they are the calibration cases for the toolkit: any later
search whose machinery disagrees with them has a bug.

**Proposition 3.**  At rank 2 with relators of length at most 5, the search
reaches 31240 of the 32040 presentations with unimodular exponent-sum matrix,
so at least 31240 balanced presentations in that box are AC-equivalent to the
standard one.  The 800 remaining ones all have both relators of length 5 and all
of them are reached once the length bound is 6.  This computation does not
decide the 800.  It shows only that the length bound, and not the conjugator
bound, is what keeps them out of Search B.

## What is not established, and where the toolkit stops

* Nothing here says anything about AK(3) or about any member of the
  Akbulut-Kirby family.  The toolkit encodes AK(3) as
  `(xyxYXY, xxxYYYY)` and checks that its exponent-sum matrix
  `[[1, -1], [3, -4]]` is unimodular, which is a necessary condition for the
  presented group to be trivial.  The relators have lengths 6 and 7, far outside
  the bounds of every search recorded here, and the toolkit does not attempt
  them.
* A presentation that the search does not reach is not thereby known to be not
  AC-equivalent to the standard one.  The bounds restrict the vertex set and the
  edge set, so absence from a search is a statement about the bounded graph.  The
  single exception is the determinant test, which is unconditional.
* The Smith normal form of the exponent-sum matrix is an invariant and it is not
  a complete one.  The toolkit has no other invariant.
* No claim is made about the Andrews-Curtis conjecture in either direction.

## Citations

The toolkit encodes one family from the literature, and the naming follows
these two papers.  Both were checked against the publisher record.

* J. J. Andrews and M. L. Curtis, "Free groups and handlebodies", Proceedings of
  the American Mathematical Society 16 (1965), 192-195,
  DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-1965-0173241-8.  This is the paper that states the
  conjecture.
* S. Akbulut and R. Kirby, "A potential smooth counterexample in dimension 4 to
  the Poincare conjecture, the Schoenflies conjecture, and the Andrews-Curtis
  conjecture", Topology 24 (1985), no. 4, 375-390,
  DOI 10.1016/0040-9383(85)90010-2.  This is the paper whose presentation
  xyx = yxy with a power relation is the origin of the family that `ac.py`
  writes as `ak_presentation(k)`, namely xyx = yxy together with
  x^k = y^{k+1}.  The paper states that the group of its own presentation is
  trivial and that trivialising it by Andrews-Curtis moves seems difficult.
  Nothing in this toolkit depends on that statement, and the toolkit does not
  check it.
