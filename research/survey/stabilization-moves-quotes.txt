   4| \section{Stable Andrews--Curtis conjecture and unknot diagrams}\label{sec:stable}
   5| 
   6| Our focus in this work has so far been on the standard Andrews--Curtis conjecture. In this section, we turn our attention to a closely related variant, known as the “stable” or “weak” Andrews--Curtis conjecture, which is also of great interest, not least because of its connections to algebraic topology. (See e.g. \cite{Meier2016,Bagherifard2021} for recent work on this connection.)
   7| 
   8| \subsection{Stable Andrews--Curtis conjecture} \label{subsec:stable}
   9| 
  10| The stable Andrews--Curtis conjecture, also called the weak Andrews--Curtis conjecture, allows for two further moves in addition to the standard AC-moves (AC1)--(AC3), for the purpose of trivializing a presentation. Namely,
  11| \begin{enumerate}[label=(AC\arabic*)]
  12| 	\setcounter{enumi}{3}
  13| 	\item Include a new generator and a trivial relator, i.e. replace $\angles{x_1, \dots, x_n \mid r_1, \dots, r_n}$ by $\angles{x_1, \dots, x_n, x_{n+1} \mid r_1, \dots, r_n, x_{n+1}}$.
  14| 	\item Remove a trivial relator and the corresponding generator, i.e. the inverse of (AC4).
  15| \end{enumerate}
  16| 
  17| Two balanced presentations that can be related by a sequence of transformations (AC1)--(AC5) are said to be \textit{stably AC-equivalent}. A presentation that is stably AC-equivalent to a trivial presentation is referred to as \emph{stably AC-trivial}. The stable Andrews--Curtis conjecture states that every balanced presentation is stably AC-trivial.
  18| 
  19| As the conjecture allows for addition and removal of new generators and trivial relators, the following lemma is quite useful.
  20| 
  21| \begin{lemma}[Substitution and Removal]\label{lem:substitution}
  22| 	Let $P=\langle x_1,\ldots, x_n, y \mid r_1,\ldots, r_n, y^{-1}w\rangle$ be a presentation of the trivial group, where $w$ is a word in $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. Then $P'=\langle x_1,\ldots, x_n \mid r_1',\ldots, r_n'\rangle$ is stably AC-equivalent to $P$, where $r_i'$ is $r_i$ with all occurrences of $y$ replaced by $w$.
  23| \end{lemma}
  24| 
  25| \begin{proof}
  26| 	First, we substitute $w$ for $y$ in $r_1,\ldots,r_n$, giving $\widetilde{P}=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n,y\mid r_1',\ldots,r_n',y^{-1}w\rangle$ (cf. \cref{def:ac-sub}). Since $r_1',\ldots,r_n'$ do not contain $y$, we can define the surjective homomorphism $\phi:\widetilde{P}\longrightarrow P'$ by $\phi(x_i)=x_i$ and $\phi(y)=w$. Since $\widetilde{P}$ is trivial, $P'$ is trivial. Therefore, the normal closure of $r_1',\ldots, r_n'$ gives the free group generated by $x_1,\ldots, x_n$, which means $w$ can be written as a product of conjugates of the $r'_i$, namely, $w=w_1 (r_{i_1}')^{\pm1}w_1^{-1}w_2 (r_{i_2}')^{\pm 1}w_2^{-1}\cdots w_m (r_{i_m}')^{\pm 1}w_m^{-1}$, for words $w_i$ in the $x_i$ and $i_k \in \{1,\ldots,n\}$. Note that $m$ may be much larger than $n$. Now using (AC1)--(AC3) we can transform  $y^{-1}w$ to $w_1 (r_{i_1}')^{\pm1}w_1^{-1}\cdots w_m (r_{i_m}')^{\pm 1}w_m^{-1}w^{-1}y$ which equals $y$, and then we can remove $y$ by (AC5).
  27| \end{proof}
  28| 
  29| We note that, much like the substitution move in the standard Andrews--Curtis conjecture, the \emph{substitution and removal} move can be interpreted as a supermove in the case of stable Andrews--Curtis conjecture. For substitution, the number of AC moves constituting the supermove is a linear function of the lengths of the relators (see \cref{sec:AC}). In contrast, our proof of \cref{lem:substitution} does not yield a similar result for the \emph{substitution and removal} move due to the absence of a bound on $m$. Finding this bound, or discovering an alternative proof of the lemma that establishes such a bound on the number of AC moves packaged into this supermove, would be very useful.

  38| \subsubsection{Stably AC-Trivial Presentations From Wirtinger Presentations}
  39| 
  40| We recall briefly the construction of the Wirtinger presentations of a knot group. Given any oriented knot diagram with $n$ crossings, we assign generators $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^n$ to each of the arcs, and each crossing gives us a relator $\{r_i\}_{i=1}^n$. In our notation, the left crossing in \cref{fig:wp} corresponds to the relator $y=x^{-1}zx$ and the right one gives $y=xzx^{-1}$. These generators and relators form a balanced presentation of the fundamental group of the knot complement. Moreover, there exists an ordering of the relators and a choice of $\pm 1$ as exponents such that the relators satisfy the equation
  41| $$\prod_{i=1}^nr_i^{\pm1}=1.$$
  42| Thus any relator $r_k$ can be eliminated, giving $\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\mid r_1,\ldots,r_{k-1},r_{k+1},\ldots,r_n\rangle$, without changing the underlying group.

 300| \begin{definition}[Stable Andrews--Curtis Moves]
 301| Let $P = \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \mid r_1, \dots, r_n \rangle$ be a balanced group presentation. The \emph{stable Andrews--Curtis moves} are the following:
 302| \begin{enumerate}
 303|   \item[\textbf{(AC1)}] Replace $r_i$ with $r_ir_j$ for some $j\neq i$
 304|   \item[\textbf{(AC2)}] Replace $r_i$ with $r_i^{-1}$
 305|   \item[\textbf{(AC3)}] Replace $r_i$ with $gr_ig^{-1}$ where $g$ is a generator or its inverse
 306|   \item[\textbf{(AC4)}] Add a new generator $x_{n+1}$ and trivial relator $r_{n+1}=x_{n+1}$
 307|   \item[\textbf{(AC5)}] Remove a trivial relator and the generator, i.e., the inverse of (AC4)
 308| \end{enumerate}
 309| The stable Andrews--Curtis conjecture allows for two additional moves beyond the usual AC-moves (AC1)--(AC3).
 310| 
 311| Two presentations are said to be \emph{stably Andrews--Curtis equivalent} if one can be obtained from the other by a finite sequence of moves (AC1)--(AC5).
 312| 
 313| A presentation is said to be \emph{stably Andrews--Curtis trivial} if it is stably Andrews--Curtis equivalent to the trivial presentation $\langle \,\,\, \mid \,\,\, \rangle$.
 314| \end{definition}
